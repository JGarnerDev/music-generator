# Medieval

A period lean rather than a genre or a mood: it says *when*, and leaves the
groove and the key to the layers around it.

## Harmony

The defining absence is the **leading tone**. Functional harmony — the pull of a
major V back to i — was not yet how music worked, so a harmonic-minor cadence
instantly sounds four hundred years too late. Write `VII` (flat, major, no pull)
and let phrases end by simply arriving.

- `mode: dorian` for the natural sixth that makes it sound old rather than sad.
  Mixolydian works equally well; both are read as minor- and major-idiom
  respectively by the blend.
- **Open fifths, no thirds.** The third was a dissonance for most of this period.
  A bare fifth is the single most effective move available here.
- Drones. One held note underneath everything, usually the tonic, occasionally
  the fifth. Harmony as a *departure from* the drone rather than a sequence.

## Instrumentation

Plucked and blown: lute (`pluck`), recorder or shawm (`lead`), a hand drum, a
bowed drone. No piano — a piano is three centuries out — so if the blend picks
one, override with [`nylon-guitar`](../timbre/nylon-guitar.md) or the pluck
voices. [`choir`](../timbre/choir.md) for plainsong.

## Rhythm

Either unmeasured and free (plainsong: no kit at all, long notes, no barline
you could clap to) or a strong dance pulse in 6/8 — `jig-lilt` — with a frame
drum. There is very little between those two.
