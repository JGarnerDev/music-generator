# Taste

Rules for composing here, derived from [studies](./studies.md) — attempts at a
musical concept that were played and thumbed up or down.

**This file is the only record.** A study is scratch: it is fanned out, rendered,
judged, distilled into a rule here, and then *deleted* — files and audio both.
So a rule has to stand on its own, in terms of fields and knobs this repo has,
without pointing at a study, a set name, an id or an mp3. If a rule needs an
artifact to be understood, it is not written well enough yet.

What survives from a study is the finding: the concept, the axis, which way the
thumbs went, and what to do about it. That is enough to argue with a rule, and
enough to overturn one — run the same concept and axis again and see whether it
still holds.

## How a rule gets written

A rule goes in when a tag has landed the same way **four or five times** on the
same concept. Fewer than that is an anecdote — a mood on a particular afternoon —
and writing it down would harden a coincidence into a house style, which is
precisely the failure `docs/variety.md` exists about. A rule below that bar can
still be recorded, marked **provisional**, so long as it says so.

Format, one section per rule:

```markdown
### <the rule, as an instruction>

<one paragraph: what to do, concretely, in terms of fields this repo has.>

**From:** `<concept>` × `<axis>` — `<tag>` ×N up / ×M down, <n> attempts.
<which values won and lost.>
```

No filenames, no set names, no ids. Those stop existing.

## Standing preferences

Things stated directly rather than derived from a tally. They are here because
they steer composition the same way a rule does, and they are marked as
unevidenced so a study can later confirm or overturn them.

### Two lead lines, not one

Write leads in pairs — harmonised, answering, or trading — rather than a single
top line over a bed. Pieces with one lead have read as thin and directionless.
Concretely: two `lead` tracks with different `pan`, or a `lead` and a `pluck`
answering across bars rather than sharing them.

**From:** stated directly, not yet studied. The `twin-leads` and `call-response`
concepts exist to put evidence under it.

## Derived rules

### For a chorus lift, commit the register — don't sit in the middle

Take `register` to `subterranean` or `high`. The middle two read as `thin`: `low`
and `mid` fold the roots into the same crowded octave as the pads and pluck, so
the section gets busier without lifting. Pick an extreme and let the gap do the
work.

**From:** `chorus-lift` × `register` — `thin` ×0 up / ×2 down, 4 attempts.
Subterranean up, low down, mid down, high up. **Provisional** — one set, below
the four-or-five threshold above. Running the same concept and axis on a
different mood confirms or kills it.

## Worth studying next

Ordered by how much a verdict would change:

- `chorus-lift` × `register`, a second mood — confirm or kill the provisional
  rule above.
- `negative-space` × `density` — how empty is too empty here.
- `guitar-solo` × `phrasing` — a written axis, so it needs composing by hand,
  and it is the one most likely to produce a rule that changes every piece.
- `hook` × `note-choice` — what makes a cell hummable for this listener.
