# Romantic / Tender

Two people and no danger. A confession at a window, a dance nobody else is
watching, the moment before or just after. Distinct from
[`calm`](./calm.md), which is about *safety*: this one is warm because someone
is there, and it wants to be listened to rather than to sit under a scene.

## Direction

- **Tonality:** major, and unafraid of it — but the tenderness lives in the
  borrowed minor iv (`[IV, iv, I]`), which is the oldest trick in the book and
  still the one that catches. Major sevenths and added 9ths everywhere; a plain
  triad sounds like a statement, and this is not a statement.
- **Tempo:** 62–88 BPM. A slow dance, not a lament — there is a pulse and the
  music knows it.
- **Voicing:** close, mid-register, thirds and sixths. Two voices moving in
  parallel sixths is the sound of two people, and it is worth being that literal.
  Keep the bass simple so the inner voices can move.
- **Melody:** wide intervals taken slowly — a rising sixth, then step back down.
  Let phrases breathe and *end*, rather than running on; the silence is where the
  other person answers.
- **Dynamics:** 0.35–0.65, with real swells. Unlike `calm`, an event is welcome
  here: one phrase should be the one it was all leading to.

## Lo-fi treatment

- Warm and close. Modest reverb — a big hall makes it public, and this isn't.
  Pairs beautifully with [`tape`](../timbre/tape.md), which supplies the
  nostalgia this palette leaves deliberately unstated.
- The rub is drive: overdriven timbres fight everything above. If a scene wants
  romance with grit, use a genre for the grit and leave the timbre warm.

## Layer it with

- [`jazz`](../genre/jazz.md) for a supper-club reading — the ii–V vocabulary and
  this tonality were made for each other.
- [`folk`](../genre/folk.md) for something plainer and more sincere.
- [`nostalgic`](./nostalgic.md) is the neighbouring emotion; pick that one if the
  love is remembered rather than happening.
