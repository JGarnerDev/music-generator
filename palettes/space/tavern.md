# Tavern / Small Room

Indoors, small, and full. Layer it onto any emotion and genre; it carries no key,
no tempo and no groove.

## The room

Barely any reverb at all — a quarter of a second, and mostly early reflections
from close wooden surfaces. Bodies absorb the top end and the low ceiling means
there is no low-frequency room mode to speak of. The dominant effect is not the
tail but the **proximity**: everything sounds a metre away, because it is.

## What it does to the writing

The opposite instruction to every other space here: this room lets you be busy.

- **Density is fine.** With no tail to smear it, a fast strummed part stays legible.
  This is the one space that suits a jig, a reel, or a full kit.
- **Play in the middle.** A small room has no bottom and no air; anything written
  below the bass's normal register just disappears.
- **Loudness is uniform.** Compression is the honest model — in a crowded room
  everything is played hard enough to be heard over everything else, and dynamic
  subtlety is lost before it reaches the listener.
- **Let it be a bit rough.** Band-limited and close is what a room sounds like;
  a hi-fi tavern is a recording studio.

## Pairs with

[`folk`](../genre/folk.md), [`whimsical`](../emotion/whimsical.md),
[`calm`](../emotion/calm.md), [`blues`](../genre/blues.md). It is the correct
space for most D&D downtime, and it is the one that makes a `lofi` bed sound
wrong — the crackle implies a recording, and this implies a performance.
