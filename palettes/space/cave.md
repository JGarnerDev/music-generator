# Cave / Underground

Under the ground. Layer it onto any emotion and genre; it carries no key, no
tempo and no groove.

## The room

Long and dark, but **not** a cathedral: a cave has no parallel walls and no
proportion, so instead of a smooth tail it gives a few distinct early
reflections — a slap that comes back late and wrong — sitting inside a murky
decay. The top end is gone entirely, absorbed by damp rock. Below about 200 Hz
everything is louder than it should be.

## What it does to the writing

- **Register low.** This room flatters the bottom two octaves and swallows
  everything above 3 kHz, so a bright melody simply will not arrive. Use
  [`lonely`](../emotion/lonely.md)'s `subterranean` register.
- **Use the slap.** A discrete echo a fifth of a second later is the one thing
  that says *cave* rather than *big room*. Write phrases with space after them so
  the reflection is audible; a busy part hides the only distinguishing feature.
- **Percussion becomes a hazard.** A snare in here is a smear. Keep the kit to a
  low tom, a rim, or nothing.
- **Sustain does the work.** A held note reveals the room; a short one doesn't.

## Pairs with

[`lonely`](../emotion/lonely.md), [`mysterious`](../emotion/mysterious.md),
[`tense`](../emotion/tense.md), [`ambient`](../genre/ambient.md). For a dungeon
crawl, layer it with a drumless genre and let the space be the only rhythm.
