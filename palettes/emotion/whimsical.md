# Whimsical / Comic

Something is up to no good and nobody is in danger. A goblin market, a failed
pickpocket, a gnome inventor, the party's plan going wrong in a way everyone will
laugh about. The one palette here that is allowed to be **silly**, and the trap is
writing merely `happy` instead.

## Direction

- **Tonality:** **lydian** — major with a raised fourth. That one note is the
  entire joke: it is bright past the point of sincerity, which is why every
  cartoon and every faerie scene has reached for it for eighty years. The
  progression `[I, II, …]` gets a major II out of the mode diatonically, and that
  chord is the sound of an eyebrow going up.
- **Tempo:** 96–138 BPM. Quick, but the *comedy* is in the rhythm rather than the
  speed: leave gaps for things to fall over in.
- **Voicing:** thin and staccato. Two voices, short notes, a lot of rests. Density
  is the enemy — a full pad turns mischief into adventure.
- **Melody:** leaps, and the wrong ones. A sixth up followed by a seventh down
  reads as a stumble; do it twice and it reads as a character. Grace notes and
  quick turns. End phrases on the raised fourth and refuse to resolve.
- **Dynamics:** wide and abrupt, 0.25–0.9. A sudden quiet bar is funnier than a
  loud one.

## Direction — rhythm

Take a figure with a limp: `3+3+2` for something lopsided, `pushed-eighths` for
nervous scurrying, `jig-lilt` in 6/8 for capering. The even `gallop` is a chase
and this is not one.

## Lo-fi treatment

- Bright and dry. This is the palette that fights the house lo-fi bed hardest —
  a warm 2 kHz low-pass makes a joke sound wistful. Push the cutoff up and keep
  the reverb short.
- [`music-box`](../timbre/music-box.md) and the pluck voices suit it; anything
  sustained and warm does not.

## Layer it with

- [`folk`](../genre/folk.md) for a market or a tavern.
- [`ragtime`](../genre/ragtime.md) for outright comedy.
- Pair with a `waltz` meter for something toy-like and slightly sinister.
