# Celtic

A jig in 6/8, and — as importantly — **mixolydian**: major with a flat seventh.
That one note is why a tune sounds Irish rather than merely folky, and why the
`VII` chord (a whole tone below the tonic, major) appears in every progression
here instead of a V.

## Groove

- **Twelve steps to the bar**, felt as two groups of three. The `jig-lilt` figure
  states exactly that.
- **Tempo:** 104–140 BPM. A reel is the same music in 4/4 — for that, drop the
  meter and use straight eighths.
- **The bodhrán, not a kit.** A low tom on the two dotted beats with a rattle
  between; no backbeat anywhere. A snare on 2 and 4 destroys the lilt instantly.
- **Drones.** A held tonic or fifth under everything, from a pipe or a fiddle's
  open string. Harmony as departure from the drone rather than a progression.

## Melody

Continuous eighth notes, almost no rests, moving mostly stepwise with occasional
rolls (a grace note above and below). The tune does not stop for breath — that
relentlessness is the dance.

Layer with [`happy`](../emotion/happy.md), [`hopeful`](../emotion/hopeful.md) or
[`medieval`](../era/medieval.md), and with
[`nylon-guitar`](../timbre/nylon-guitar.md) or the whistle voices on the `lead`
