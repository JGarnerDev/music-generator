# Blues

A *groove/harmony* layer, not a mood. Pair with an emotion for key + feeling
(`sad` × `blues` = a slow burn; `angry` × `blues` = a dirty stomp; `lonely` ×
`blues` = the classic). The first progression is the full 12-bar; the second is
the quick-change variant; the third is an 8-bar minor blues.

## Groove

- **Tempo:** 60–120 BPM. Slow blues at 60–72 is the most expressive; 100+ turns
  into a shuffle or a jump.
- **Feel:** shuffled 8ths (triplet subdivision, ~62%) — the swing is heavier than
  jazz's. Bass walks or pumps a boogie figure; chords land on the beat, behind it
  slightly. Leave the fourth beat of each phrase open for a fill.
- **Harmony:** I–IV–V, and that's genuinely all. Every chord is a dominant 7th
  including the tonic, which is what makes it *blues* and not folk — the engine
  voices triads, so add the b7 in the melody instead. `mode: either`: major over a
  minor-pentatonic melody is the signature clash; true minor blues is darker and
  drops the trick.
- **Melody:** minor pentatonic plus the b5 over a major-ish backing. Bends and
  slides implied by chromatic approach notes. Call-and-response: play a phrase,
  leave a bar, answer it. Repeat the same line three times — that's the form.
- **Instrumentation:** piano comping or a driven pluck as the guitar, bass on the
  boogie figure. Sparse — the space between phrases is the genre.
