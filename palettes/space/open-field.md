# Open Field / Outdoors

Outside, with nothing near enough to reflect. Layer it onto any emotion and
genre; it carries no key, no tempo and no groove.

## The room

There isn't one, and that is the effect. Outdoors there is no tail — sound
radiates away and never returns — so what you get is a very dry direct signal
with a faint, very distant reflection from whatever is on the horizon. The
counter-intuitive part is that **dry sounds bigger here**, because the absence of
a room is what the ear reads as space. A reverb tail would put walls back.

## What it does to the writing

- **Dry, and wide.** Stereo width does the work reverb usually does. Pan parts
  properly rather than reaching for a hall.
- **Sparse.** With nothing filling the gaps, silence is genuinely silent, and that
  emptiness is the whole character. Fewer notes than feels comfortable.
- **The top end survives.** Unlike every other space here, nothing is absorbing
  the treble, so a bright whistle or a high string carries. This is why the
  western lead whistle sounds the way it does.
- **The low end doesn't.** No room means no bass reinforcement; a part written to
  rumble indoors sounds thin outdoors, and correcting for it is the point.
