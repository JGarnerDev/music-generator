# Reggae / Dub

A *groove/harmony* layer, not a mood. Pair with an emotion for key + feeling
(`calm` × `reggae` = sunny downtime; `hopeful` × `reggae` = roots uplift;
`mysterious` × `dub`-leaning voicing = a heavy, echoing crawl).

Note this one **works without a drum kit**: the defining element is the *skank* —
a chord instrument on the upbeats — not a percussion pattern. It renders today.

## Groove

- **Tempo:** 62–92 BPM. Roots reggae sits 70–80; rocksteady a touch slower; ska
  is the same feel played fast, at the very top.
- **Feel:** the **skank** — short, clipped chord stabs on the *upbeats* (the
  "and" of every beat), epiano or muted pluck, with beat 1 left conspicuously
  empty. That hole on the downbeat is the whole genre. The bass fills it: a
  melodic, syncopated line that is the lead voice, not the foundation — write it
  like a tune and keep it in the low register.
- **Harmony:** two chords, often just I and IV, cycled indefinitely. `mode:
  either` — major reads sunny, minor reads roots/dub-heavy. Slow harmonic rhythm;
  never a busy change. No extensions beyond the occasional 7th.
- **Instrumentation:** epiano on the skank, bass carrying the melody, pluck
  doubling the skank an octave up or answering it, pad only for dub washes.
- **Dub treatment:** strip elements out and drop them back in; drench the stabs
  in long feedback echo (a dotted-8th delay) and let the tail smear. Subtraction
  is the arrangement technique.
