# Flamenco

A *groove/harmony* layer, not a mood. Pair with an emotion for key + feeling
(`angry` × `flamenco` = the fast, hammered bulería energy; `sad` × `flamenco` =
the cante jondo lament; `tense` × `flamenco` = the duel in a courtyard).

**Modal by default.** Set the emotion's `scale: phrygian` for the real thing —
the flat 2nd a semitone above the tonic is the sound, and in phrygian it's
diatonic rather than borrowed, so the melody uses it too. Against a plain `minor`
emotion the `bII` still resolves (it's written with its accidental) but the tune
won't touch that note, which is half the genre missing.

## Groove

- **Tempo:** 96–138 BPM. The lament sits at the bottom and is rubato in
  practice; the rumba/bulería side drives at the top.
- **Feel:** palmas and a cajón, no kit. Claps on 2 and 4, cajón bass in a 3–3–2
  tresillo, soft slaps filling the eighths. The pulse should feel handmade —
  people in a semicircle, not a machine.
- **Harmony:** the **Andalusian cadence** (i–VII–VI–V) is the spine: a stepwise
  fall to a *major* V that never resolves back to i but simply starts again. That
  hanging dominant is the tension. The other move is the phrygian bII a semitone
  above the tonic — write it explicitly (`bII`), and voice it in first inversion
  over the tonic bass so the semitone rubs.
- **Instrumentation:** pluck is the nylon guitar and does everything — chords,
  the falseta between vocal phrases, the rasgueado. Bass on roots only, sparse.
  Pad for a held vocal note. Piano is a poor fit here; leave it out of the mix or
  use it for a single answering line.
- **Rasgueado:** the fast fanned strum — approximate it with a dense sixteenth
  arp on the same chord that accelerates into the downbeat, then a full stop.
  Silence right after a flurry is as important as the flurry.
- **Gated on meter:** the 12-beat *compás* (bulería, soleá) with its accents on
  12, 3, 6, 8, 10 is flamenco's real clock and needs time-signature support (see
  progress.md). What's here is the 4/4 rumba flamenca side.
