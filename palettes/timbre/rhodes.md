# Rhodes / Electric Piano

A tine struck by a hammer and read by a pickup: a tuning fork wired to an
amplifier. Its whole character is that it is **velocity-dependent in a way a
synth is not** — soft it is a glassy bell, hard it barks and breaks up, and the
distance between those two sounds is the instrument.

## Sound

- **Source:** the `epiano` voice. Write real dynamics; a Rhodes played at one
  velocity is a sine wave with a name.
- **Signal chain:** light compression → chorus (the stereo vibrato in the
  suitcase amp, and the single most recognisable thing about the sound) → tape
  echo → a small room. Chorus **before** the echo, so the echo repeats a chorused
  signal rather than smearing the chorus.
- **Character:** hollow, woody, mid-forward. Very little top end and almost no
  fundamental — a Rhodes chord is mostly second and third harmonic, which is why
  it sits in a mix without fighting anything.

## When to reach for it

[`jazz`](../genre/jazz.md), [`lofi`](../genre/lofi.md),
[`soul`](../genre/soul.md), [`nostalgic`](../emotion/nostalgic.md),
[`romantic`](../emotion/romantic.md). The default keys voice for anything after
1965 that isn't a rock band.

## How to voice it

Rootless voicings — leave the root to the bass and play thirds, sevenths and
extensions in the middle. That is how the instrument is actually played, and it
is why a Rhodes comping plain triads sounds student-ish.
