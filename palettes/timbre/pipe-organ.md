# Pipe Organ

Air pushed through pipes. The defining thing about an organ is what it *cannot*
do: there is no touch sensitivity, no decay, and no dynamic within a note. A key
is down or it is not. Everything expressive has to come from which notes and
which stops, which is why organ music sounds the way it does.

## Sound

- **Source:** the `pad` voice with an instant attack and no release — the exact
  opposite of the string bed it otherwise resembles.
- **Signal chain:** compression → a long hall → widening. The room is not
  optional: an organ was built for a particular building, and a dry one sounds
  like an accordion. Layer [`palettes/space/cathedral`](../space/cathedral.md).
- **Character:** a *mixture* stop sounds several octaves and a fifth above the
  played note at once, which is why an organ chord is so much brighter than its
  notes suggest. Double the top of a voicing an octave and a twelfth up to get it.

## When to reach for it

[`solemn`](../emotion/solemn.md), [`baroque`](../genre/baroque.md),
[`epic`](../emotion/epic.md), a cathedral, a villain with taste.

## How to voice it

Use the pedal: a bass line an octave or two below everything else, moving slowly
and independently of the hands. That separation is what makes an organ sound
immense rather than merely loud. **Hold the notes** — an organ line with gaps in
it is an organ line with the wind cut off, and it is the one articulation the
instrument really has. Terraced dynamics only; no swells.
