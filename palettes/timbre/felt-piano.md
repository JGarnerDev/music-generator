# Felt Piano

A piano with a strip of felt between the hammers and the strings. The attack
goes, the sustain thins, and what is left is mostly the *sound of the mechanism*
— the thud of the hammer, the damper lifting, the player breathing. The
defining lo-fi keyboard sound of the last decade, and the fastest way to make a
piano sound like it is in a room with you rather than on a stage.

## Sound

- **Source:** the `piano` voice, soft. Every velocity below about 0.6 — this
  instrument physically cannot be played loud, and writing it loud is the one
  mistake that gives it away.
- **Signal chain:** gentle compression (brings the mechanism up under the note) →
  a low-pass around 2.5 kHz (the felt is doing this in the real world) → a small
  room, not a hall. Close and dry: the point is intimacy.
- **Character:** dark, breathy, uneven. Notes in the low register thud; the top
  two octaves almost disappear, which is why this voice lives in the middle.

## When to reach for it

Anything quiet and close — [`calm`](../emotion/calm.md),
[`nostalgic`](../emotion/nostalgic.md), [`romantic`](../emotion/romantic.md), a
`lofi` study loop. It is the opposite of a concert grand and should never be
asked to carry a crescendo.

## How to voice it

Mid-register, close position, no wide spreads — a tenth sounds like two separate
thuds. Let notes overlap slightly; the blur is the sound. Pair with
[`tape`](./tape.md) and you have the whole lo-fi piano genre in two layers.
