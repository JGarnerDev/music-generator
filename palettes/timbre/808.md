# 808 / Sub Bass

The Roland TR-808 kick, tuned and played as a bass part: a decaying sine wave
with almost nothing above 100 Hz, held long enough to become a pitch. It is the
low end of most popular music made since 1990, and it behaves unlike any other
bass here — it does not *support* the kick, it **is** the kick.

## Sound

- **Source:** the `bass` voice, sine-dominant, long release.
- **Signal chain:** distortion **first** — an 808 that isn't clipping is inaudible
  on anything without a subwoofer, and the harmonics the clipping adds are how a
  phone conveys the note at all → a low-pass to put the grit back in its place →
  compression to hold the level.
- **Character:** enormous, slow, and slightly late with itself: the pitch of a long
  sine takes a moment to become audible, which is why 808 parts are written
  sparsely.

## When to reach for it

[`trap`](../genre/trap.md), [`hiphop`](../genre/lofi.md),
[`techno`](../genre/techno.md), or any modern scene wanting weight without a
band. Very effective under [`tense`](../emotion/tense.md) as a pulse.

## How to voice it

**Few notes, long ones.** One a bar is normal and two is busy; sixteenths turn to
mud below 60 Hz because the waves physically overlap. Glide between pitches
rather than re-articulating. And thin the kick lane out — the usual mistake is
writing an 808 *and* a kick and wondering where the low end went.
