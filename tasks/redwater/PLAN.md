# Music brief — Redwater, Act 1

For our composer. Everything below is a **request**, not a spec — if a piece pulls you
somewhere better, go there. Each request is short on purpose; ask about anything thin.

IMPORTANT - Produce music/samples/lietmotifs/loops for the following, and properly label them so that we know which products are for what work order. When Approved, put them in an appropriately named subfolder in this tasks folder.

## The world in a paragraph

**Redwater** is a fantasy frontier: a wide borderland cut by a red-tinted river, ruled badly
from far away, raided by bandits who work for a noble who won't admit it. Think western bones
in high-fantasy dress — swords, bows, and magic, no gunslingers. The party are outsiders,
cursed by a witch to belong nowhere, dropped into a dying river town called **Ashford**. They
take part-ownership of its tavern, **the Golden Lioness**, and spend the act turning a place
they landed in by accident into a home worth defending. Underneath the town, in the water,
something ancient and lonely is listening.

Tone: warm and hopeful at the hearth, dusty and dangerous outside it, with a thin cold thread
of horror carried over from where they came from.

## Battle loops

- **Tavern raid** — Opening fight: bandits looting a half-built brewhouse while barrels roll.
  Scrappy, fast, more fun than fearful. The heroes are going to win this one and it should
  sound like it.

- **The chase** — Mounted pursuit across open country after a stolen cart. Driving, wide,
  built on hoofbeat rhythm. Motion first, menace second.

- **Camp assault at dusk** — Bandit waystation, sentries, a sniper on the ridge. Two layers:
  a low tense stalking bed and a sharper combat top for when it breaks open.

- **The town burns** — Late-act raid on Ashford itself. Heavier, wilder, real danger — the
  home they built is on fire. Highest stakes music in the act.

- **Duel with a professional** — Fighting someone who is simply better organized than you.
  Cold, controlled, mechanical pulse. No chaos; this enemy is a company, not a mob.

## Scene loops

- **The Golden Lioness** — Home base. Warm, lived-in tavern music that could plausibly be
  played in the room: fiddle, hand drum, low voices. The safest sound in the campaign.

- **Ashford by day** — A frontier town hanging on. Sparse, sunlit, a little threadbare.
  Hopeful without pretending things are fine.

- **The long road** — Travel across borderlands: big sky, dust, empty distance. Slow build,
  patient, room to talk over it.

- **Building the place** — Downtime montage: hammering, hauling, planning. Optimistic and
  productive; the act's emotional payoff track.

- **The Descent** — The crossing between worlds. One falling moment: pressure, vertigo, no
  ground. Short cue rather than a long loop is fine.

- **The deep water** — Beneath the tavern, a vast ancient thing in a flooded cavern. Slow,
  huge, alien, oddly gentle. Beautiful and wrong at the same time.

- **Old blood** — Nobles, grudges, a two-generation feud over a betrayal. Stately and bitter;
  a fallen war-hymn played too slowly for comfort.

- **Night and secrets** — After dark in town: whispered deals, a discreet house, an
  interrogation in a jail cell. Low, close, unhurried, faintly unsafe.

- **Barovian residue** — The curse stirring. Thin, cold, wrong — a few seconds of it should
  make players' skin crawl. Used sparingly as a sting or an under-layer.

## Character motifs

- **Sable Wren** — Tavern owner, tiefling, warm and competent, doesn't explain herself. Steady
  and generous with a private edge underneath. The hearth, personified.

- **Asher** — The being in the deep water. Ancient, telepathic, monstrous to look at, funny
  and lonely once you know him. Should feel enormous, patient, and — surprisingly — kind.

- **The Dream Pastry Woman** — The hag who cursed the party and vanished laughing. Sweet
  surface over rot: a lullaby fragment that sours. Recurs across the whole campaign.

- **Lady Sorcha Marlow** — Widow-cold noble running the raids from behind a clean hand.
  Elegant, patient, merciless. Charm as a weapon; nothing warm anywhere in it.

- **Lord Corren Cael** — The good lord who is losing. Proud, honest, out-matched, grieving a
  grandfather who died for the house that betrayed him. Noble theme worn thin.

- **Harrow Bane** — Bandit captain, ex-military, runs raiding like a business. A name the town
  goes quiet at. Disciplined, unhurried, no cruelty for show — which is worse.

- **Nella** — Hunter and marksman. Escapes twice before we ever catch her. Light, watchful,
  always at a distance — heard before she's seen, gone before it resolves.

- **Jorn** — Exiled goliath druid raiding to protect his own patch of wild. Heavy, earthy,
  half-sad. Loyal to his crew, not the cause.

- **Wick** — Elf swordlock riding with bandits as cover for a guild monster hunt. Detached,
  precise, clinical. Not evil, not on our side.

- **Mara Tull** — Town sheriff who buried both her children and still stands watch. Grief
  turned to steel. Plain, unshowy, unbreakable.

- **The Unhoming (party theme)** — Our main title. Four strangers cursed to belong nowhere,
  building a home anyway. Should carry both halves: exile and hearth. Everything else can
  quote it.

## Location motifs

- **The Golden Lioness** — The building itself, half old and half raw new timber. A short warm
  phrase we can hear grow richer as the party builds the place up over the act.

- **Ashford** — Ford town on a tributary, dying slowly, worth saving. Small and stubborn.
  Ideally the same phrase can play threadbare early and full late.

- **The Redwater** — The red river the whole region is named for. Wide, ancient, indifferent;
  its color is older than the blood spilled in it. Flowing, unresolved.

- **Duskwater** — The rival fief the raids come out of. Colder, wealthier, closed. The same
  world as Ashford, but no welcome in it.

## Nice to have

- A short **victory / hearth sting** for when the party wins something and gets home.
- A **failure sting** that is sad rather than punishing — this campaign never kills the mood.
