# Cinematic / Score

A *groove/harmony* layer, not a mood — it supplies *shape*. Pair with an emotion
for key + feeling (`tense` × `cinematic` = a stalking scene; `epic` × `cinematic`
= the trailer moment; `mysterious` × `cinematic` = the reveal). The workhorse
layer for scoring a D&D scene.

## Groove

- **Tempo:** 60–132 BPM. Underscore sits low; a trailer build sits 90–110 and
  feels faster than it is because the subdivision doubles.
- **Feel:** an **ostinato** is the engine — a short repeating figure (pluck or
  piano, 8ths or 16ths) that runs unchanged while everything above it moves. Over
  a pedal tone it creates tension without a chord change. Build by *adding
  layers*, not by speeding up.
- **Harmony:** slow harmonic rhythm, one chord per bar or slower. Minor with a
  VI–VII lift is the modern-score default; major I–V–vi–IV is the heroic one.
  `mode: either`. Pedal the tonic in the bass under moving upper voices — the
  friction is the drama. Avoid jazzy extensions; open 5ths and octaves read as
  scale.
- **Instrumentation:** pad as strings (the sustained bed), piano for a lone
  motif, bass doubling at the octave for weight, pluck on the ostinato.
- **Shape:** this layer wants sections. Start with the ostinato alone, add the
  pad, add the motif, then drop everything for one bar before the peak — the drop
  is what sells the arrival.
