# Baroque

A period lean rather than a genre or a mood. Where [`medieval`](./medieval.md) is
defined by the *absence* of functional harmony, this is the era that invented it
and then used nothing else.

## Harmony

- **The V is major and it means it.** Every phrase drives to a cadence; the
  harmonic-minor dominant is correct and essential here. `[i, iv, V, i]` is the
  whole period in four chords.
- **Sequences.** A two-bar idea restated a step lower, then a step lower again,
  is the most characteristic gesture in the style and the cheapest way to sound
  Baroque. Write the progression to descend by seconds or by fifths.
- **The bass moves.** Continuo means the bass line is a *melody*, walking in
  steady eighths under everything. A pedal-point bass is the wrong century.

## Texture

Counterpoint, not accompaniment: two or three independent lines each of which
would be worth listening to alone, rather than a tune over chords. In practical
terms — give the bass and the top line different rhythms, and make the middle
voice move when the others hold.

## Instrumentation

Harpsichord (`pluck` or a bright `piano` — note it has **no dynamics**, so an
even velocity is authentic rather than lazy),
[`strings`](../timbre/strings.md), [`pipe-organ`](../timbre/pipe-organ.md).
Ornament everything: trills and mordents on every long note, which in this engine
means grace notes a sixteenth before the beat.

## Rhythm

Steady and unswung, with a clear dance metre — many Baroque forms *are* dances.
3/4 for a sarabande or minuet, 6/8 for a gigue, 4/4 for an allemande. No kit at
all; the rhythm lives in the continuo.
