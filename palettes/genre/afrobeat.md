# Afrobeat

Several simple parts, each in a different place, none of which is the beat. The
groove is what happens *between* them — nobody plays the downbeat and everybody
implies it. Long: a real afrobeat track spends five minutes on one chord before
anything changes, and that patience is the form rather than a lack of ideas.

## Groove

- **Tempo:** 98–128 BPM.
- **Interlocking, not layered.** Each lane above avoids the others' hits. Writing
  a new part means finding the gaps, which is the opposite of the usual instinct
  to double the existing groove.
- **One chord.** `[i, i, i, i]` is not a placeholder here. Harmony is a colour the
  track sits in; the movement is rhythmic and it is in the guitar and the bass.
- **Guitar as percussion.** Very short, very high, one or two notes repeated in a
  cycle that is a different length from the bar, so it rotates against it.

## Layer it with

[`happy`](../emotion/happy.md), [`hopeful`](../emotion/hopeful.md), or
[`angry`](../emotion/angry.md) for the political edge the music actually has.
[`nylon-guitar`](../timbre/nylon-guitar.md) suits the guitar parts better than a
driven tone.
