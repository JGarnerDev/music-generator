# Cathedral

Where the music is, rather than what it is or how it feels. Layer it onto any
emotion and genre; it carries no key, no tempo and no groove.

## The room

Very long reverberation — four to six seconds — from hard stone with almost
nothing in it to absorb sound. The low end is enormous and the top is not, since
stone reflects bass better than treble. Crucially the reverb is **louder than the
source** at any distance: in a real nave you hear the room more than the
instrument.

## What it does to the writing

This is the whole point of `space` being a palette kind rather than a reverb
setting: a room changes what you can *write*, not just how it sounds.

- **Slow the harmony down.** At five seconds of tail, a chord change every bar
  means three chords sounding at once. One change every two or four bars is what
  the building will actually let you hear.
- **Thin the texture.** Two voices in a cathedral sound like six. A full
  arrangement turns to porridge before the second bar.
- **Leave gaps.** The silences fill themselves in, and they are the most
  characteristic sound the space makes.
- **Avoid fast rhythm entirely.** No sixteenths, no busy kit. A cathedral is a
  low-pass filter with a five-second memory.
